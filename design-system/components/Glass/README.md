# Glass
Liquid glass: a tinted gradient over a backdrop that is blurred, saturated and, in Chromium on a desktop, bent along the rim like the curved edge of a lens; a rim light and a sheen lit from the top left; and motion that squashes, lifts and settles.

- Recipe (`.bet-glass` applies all of it to any element, so does `data-bet-glass`): `background: linear-gradient(180deg, glass-a, glass-b)`, `backdrop-filter: blur(16px) saturate(1.8)`, `shadow-glass-sm` (`shadow-glass` for the top bar), the `::before` rim (a 1px ring: a steady glint top left and bottom right, plus `glass-rim` nearest the light at `--lg-x`, `--lg-y`) and the `::after` sheen (`glass-rim-soft` under the same light, a brighter band along the top edge, shade along the bottom).
- The bend (`Bet.glass`, wired by `Bet.init`): an SVG filter drawn to the element's exact size. The middle stays frosted; the outer part of the rim shows a clearer backdrop pulled inward, with red, green and blue bent by slightly different amounts, so the edge splits colour like real glass. The tint thins to 82% under it (`data-bet-bent`). Chromium on a fine pointer at 768px and up; Safari and Firefox keep the frost.
- The light: every glass element's rim light and sheen are lit from the top left and stay there; they do not follow the pointer.
- Motion: a glass button or pill lifts 3% on hover, squashes to 95% when pressed and springs back (`--ease-spring`); a toast and a menu land like a drop (`bet-glass-in`); the top bar flexes with the speed of the scroll under it and settles.
- `data-bet-glass="still"` keeps the frost, the light and the spring but skips the bend: for many small pieces that move every frame (a carousel of pills), and for a pane that changes size (the app sidebar, a panel's tab), since the bend is drawn to an exact size.
- Use it for what floats over the page or controls it: the top bar or the app's sidebar, the second button, feature pills, the search bar and filter pills, a numbers strip that opens a view (it may bend too, being small and a fixed size), the status bar, menus and open dropdowns (see Menu), toasts, and the tab left behind when a panel slides away.
- Content is solid: job cards, news panels, goals, stage tiles, tables, Bet's card, empty states, forms. Glass on all of them was tried and read as overused.
- Glass reads best over content or the colour field, where there is something to bend; on a plain page it reads as a soft pill, which is fine.
